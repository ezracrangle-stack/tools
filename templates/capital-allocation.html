Sources and uses

Cash comes from the business (operations and asset sales) or from claimholders (new debt and new shares). It goes back into the business (capex, acquisitions, working capital, and intangible spending that runs through the income statement) or back to claimholders (dividends, buybacks and debt repaid). That's Exhibit 4 of the paper, and it's the first step of their checklist.

Most US companies fund themselves. Mauboussin and Callahan read that both ways: high returns mean little need for outside money, but internal money also skips the scrutiny lenders and new shareholders would apply.

What each $1 of new sales cost

Alfred Rappaport's value drivers put every kind of investment on the same scale: how much the company spent for each $1 of new sales. If working capital grew $200 while sales grew $1,000, the working capital rate is 20%. Capex of $500 with $200 of depreciation is a fixed capital rate of 30%. Negative rates mean that line was a source of cash, like capex below depreciation.

The paper's two examples show why the split matters. Over 5 years, one large software company spent about evenly across capex, intangibles and acquisitions. A networking company put almost all of its net investment into acquisitions, while its capex ran below depreciation. Same exercise, very different questions to ask management.

What went back to shareholders

Dividends are sticky: once paid, companies treat them like a fixed cost. Buybacks swing with the cycle. Comparing the range of each (highest year minus lowest) shows how much of the payout management treats as optional. The total net payout ratio for the Russell 3000 went from 50% of net income in 1985 to 64% in 2021.

Net buybacks subtract the shares the company issued, mostly to pay its people. For more on that, use the buybacks and stock comp calculator.

What it leaves out

The intangible investment that runs through SG&A and R&D isn't here, because it doesn't show up as investment in the cash flow statement. The intangibles calculator estimates it. And this is spending, not results: pair it with ROIC and ROIIC to see whether the money earned its keep.

Where the method comes from

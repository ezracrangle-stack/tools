Mauboussin's FCF

Most people work out free cash flow as operating cash flow minus capex. Michael Mauboussin and Dan Callahan show why that number flatters a lot of companies: it adds back stock pay, leaves out assets bought with leases, and hides the money spent on intangibles inside operating costs.

Copy a few lines from the cash flow statement and you get free cash flow four ways, the statement recast and the real size of the investment. Nothing you type leaves your browser.

Which companies it works for

Almost any company outside finance. It matters most for three kinds:

  • Tech and young companies that pay in stock. In the paper's data, stock pay was about 15% of sales for the smallest companies in the Russell 1000.
  • Retailers, airlines and anyone who leases big assets. Buying and leasing a plane are both investment, but only the purchase shows up in investing.
  • Businesses built on R&D, software or brands. Most of their investment sits in operating costs.

It doesn't work for banks and insurers, whose cash flow statements mix operations and financing by design.

What this calculator can't tell you

How much of SG&A is investment

The split between spending that builds the business and spending that keeps it running is a judgment call, and it changes by industry and by company. The paper's percentages for Amazon are a starting point to argue with.

Whether the investment pays

Negative free cash flow is fine, even welcome, when the return on the investment is good. This page tells you how big the investment is. Whether it earns its keep is a question for the return on capital.

The other changes a valuation needs

The paper notes that an unlevered DCF needs more than these moves, starting with a proper NOPAT. Free cash flow, properly defined as NOPAT minus investment, doesn't change with any of them.

Where the method comes from

Michael J. Mauboussin and Dan Callahan, "Categorizing for Clarity: Cash Flow Statement Adjustments to Improve Insight," Counterpoint Global Insights (Morgan Stanley Investment Management), October 6, 2021. Their case study is Amazon in 2020.

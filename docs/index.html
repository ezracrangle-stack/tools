Ezra's Notes · Tools

Calculators for value investors, built from the spreadsheets I use to check a stock.

Free, and they'll stay free. No sign-up. Everything runs in your browser, so nothing you type gets saved anywhere.

Each one follows a book or a paper, names it, and is checked against an Excel version before it goes live. Why I built them.

  • Net-net calculator

    Live

    Graham's NCAV, modern NCAV, net cash, Schloss' working capital and book value, per share and against the price.

  • Earnings power value

    Live

    What the business is worth if today's cash profits never grow, how much of the price is a bet on growth, and the most you should pay.

  • Greenwald's moat test

    Live

    Greenwald's test. What it would cost to rebuild the business, what its earnings are worth with no growth, and what the gap says about its moat.

  • Penman's residual income

    Live

    Anchor on the book value of the operations, then see the growth the price implies and the return you earn at today's price.

  • Graham's formulas

    Live

    Graham's growth formula, the Graham number and his seven tests for the defensive investor, as he wrote them in The Intelligent Investor.

  • Mauboussin's FCF

    Live

    Mauboussin and Callahan's fixes to the cash flow statement. Free cash flow after stock pay and leases, and how much the company really invests.

  • ROIC, ROIIC and reinvestment

    Live

    How much a business earns on its capital, what the new capital earns, how much it reinvests, and the growth that supports.

  • Reverse DCF

    Live

    How many years of above-cost returns the price already assumes, and the sales growth it needs. Mauboussin and Rappaport's method, with your own numbers.

  • What to pay for quality

    Live

    How much more than the market you can pay for a business that reinvests at high returns, and the P/E that still earns your target. From The Compounders.

  • Pabrai's Dhandho bet

    Live

    Write down what can happen and how likely it is. Get the odds, the chance of losing money, and how much of your bankroll the Kelly Formula says to bet. From The Dhandho Investor.

  • Peter Lynch's PEG

    Live

    The PEG and its dividend-adjusted version, the P/E without the company's net cash, and what ten years of compounding does to the price. From One Up on Wall Street.

  • Howard Marks' market temperature

    Live

    Twenty-three pairs of opposites, from lenders to investors to funds. Pick a side for each and take the market's temperature, as Howard Marks does in Mastering the Market Cycle.

  • Intangibles and earnings

    Live

    What a company really earns once its spending on brands, customers and R&D counts as investment, by industry. From Mauboussin and Callahan's Intangibles and Earnings.

  • How often does this happen?

    Live

    Check a sales forecast against how often companies of the same size actually grew that fast, from Mauboussin and Callahan's Base Rate Book.

  • What stage is this company in?

    Live

    Place a company in its life cycle stage from the signs of its cash flows, with Mauboussin and Callahan's adjustments and what happened next to companies in each stage.

  • What discount rate should you use?

    Live

    The weighted average cost of capital (WACC) step by step, with Mauboussin and Callahan's method, or your own rate set from the risk you see.

  • How much growth is priced in?

    Live

    Split a stock price into today's earnings held flat and the present value of growth opportunities (PVGO), with Mauboussin and Callahan's two methods.

  • Capital allocation calculator

    Live

    Where a company's cash came from and where it went over 5 years, what each $1 of new sales cost in investment, and how much went back to shareholders.

  • Buybacks and stock comp

    Live

    How much stock a company issues to pay its people and how much it buys back, against Mauboussin and Callahan's medians, and who wins from a buyback.

  • Measuring the moat

    Live

    Where a company's ROIC comes from, margin or turnover, against its peers, and how much market shares move in its industry. From Mauboussin and Callahan's Measuring the Moat.

  • Red flags

    Live

    Is the company dressing up its numbers? Montier's C-Score, Beneish's M-Score and four checks from Financial Shenanigans, on two years of accounts.

  • Keynes' beauty contest

    Live

    A monthly game. Pick a number from 0 to 100; the winner is closest to two-thirds of everyone's average. How far ahead of the crowd can you think?

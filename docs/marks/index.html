Howard Marks' market temperature

"For each pair, check off the one you think is most descriptive of the current market. And if you find that most of your checkmarks are in the left-hand column, hold on to your wallet." Howard Marks' guide to market assessment, from Mastering the Market Cycle, turned into a temperature you can take every month.

No numbers needed: just your read of what's going on around you. Nothing you choose leaves your browser.

How it works

Marks' point is that the risk in investing comes mostly from how other investors behave. When they're euphoric, their buying lifts prices to dangerous levels; when they're despondent, their selling takes prices to bargain lows. The guide is a disciplined way of noticing which of the two is going on.

Each pair has a hot side and a cold side. The temperature is the share of your answers on the hot side, with "in between" counting half. Above 60 Marks would lean defensive, below 40 aggressive.

Marks' two questions

How are things priced, and how are investors around us behaving? Start with the first: if valuations aren't out of line with history, the cycle is unlikely to be extended in either direction. This guide answers the second.

A trick from the book for the stance: travel a few years into the future and look back. Are you more likely to say "I wish I'd been more aggressive" or "I wish I'd been more defensive"?

What it can't tell you

When the market will turn. Marks: even the best temperature-taking can't tell us what will happen next, just the tendencies. From a high point the market can still go up, flat or down; a fall is only the more likely of the three.

The answers are judgments, so they carry your mood. Taking the reading on the same day each month, and looking at the trend, helps keep it honest.

Where the guide comes from

Howard Marks, Mastering the Market Cycle: Getting the Odds on Your Side (2018), chapter XIII, "How to Cope with Market Cycles", and chapter XIV, "Cycle Positioning". The guide first appeared in his memo "It Is What It Is" (March 2006) and in The Most Important Thing.
